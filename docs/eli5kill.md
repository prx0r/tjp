# ELI5KILL — kill conditions mirror the trade (2026-09-12, saved word for word)

The trade works while $$DemandGrowth > SupplyResponse$$ and buyers retain downstream economics
to tolerate rising input prices.

$$
KILL =
(\text{new supply} \uparrow)
+
(\text{inventory} \uparrow)
+
(\text{lead times} \downarrow)
+
(\text{substitution} \uparrow)
+
(\text{buyer ROI} \downarrow)
+
(\text{demand growth} \downarrow)
$$

Killer signal is usually NOT price falling first — price turns after the shortage already heals.

## Ten historical kills, 50 years

**1. Oil 1979–80.** Revolution removed ~4.8m bbl/day (~7%); hoarding; prices >2× Apr79–Apr80 ([Fed][1]).
Killed by: recession, conservation, non-OPEC supply, inventories, returned supply → long real decline.
Lesson: demand destruction kills before new supply arrives.

**2. DRAM 1987–89.** PCs expanding; every box needs DRAM; trade restrictions + underproduction;
256K spot ~3× in 4 months ([Academies][2]). Kill: next-gen capacity, yields, inventory reversal,
order cuts, obsolescence. Lesson: supply responds late, then all at once — prime HBM/DRAM risk today.

**3. Fiber 1996–2001.** Exponential traffic, financed buildout, then overcapacity; 2001 capex collapse ([SD][3]).
Most important AI warning: everyone can correctly identify the bottleneck and overbuild it. Internet was
real and still grew — fiber investors died because supply outran monetizable demand.

**4. Palladium 1998–2001.** Regulation indispensability + Russian disruption → ~$1,083–1,094/oz, above platinum ([USGS][4]).
Imploded on: platinum substitution, thrifting, stabilized supply, excess stockpiles → ~$326 EOY 2001,
Ford ~$1B writedown. Lesson: price ↑ ⇒ substitution R&D ↑ — decay the bottleneck score over time.

**5. China metals 2003–08.** Urbanization; steel/ore demand 5.3×/8.5×; prices +180% vs 2002 ([IMF][5])([IMF][6]).
2008 credit collapse killed it ([IMF][7]). Lesson: watch buyer financing as closely as physical demand.

**6. Uranium 2003–07.** Renaissance hopes; reactors must run; ~13× spot ([WNA][8]). Killed by new supply
(Kazakhstan) then Fukushima demand shock (−70%). Lesson: two kill paths — supply healing OR thesis destruction.

**7. Rare earths 2010–11.** China cut quotas ~70%; Nd ~10× ([SD][9]). Killed by thrifting, substitutes,
recycling, new mines, quota evaporation. Signal: by 2011 only ~half the quota was used ([CRS][10]) —
price crossed willingness-to-pay. Lesson: unconsumed allocated supply = exhaustion signal.

**8. Lumber 2020–21.** Lockdown housing value + mill cuts → vertical prices ([Fed][11]). Killed by restarts,
rates, services shift. Lesson: distinguish LeadTime_restart from LeadTime_newcapacity.

**9. Container shipping 2020–22.** Goods shift + clogged ports → 5–7× rates ([UNCTAD][12]); healed H2 2022 ([UNCTAD][13]).
Lesson: EffectiveCapacity ≠ InstalledCapacity — monitor usable, not headline, capacity everywhere.

**10. Nitrogen fertilizer 2021–22.** Gas 70–80% of ammonia cost; EU gas explosion; prices ~3× ([WB][14]).
Eased early 2023: Russian exports persisted, EU recovered, capacity grew, demand weakened ([Reuters][15]).
Lesson: recurse upstream to the true constrained primitive (gas → ammonia → nitrogen → food).

## All ten died five ways

Supply catches up (uranium, DRAM) · Demand collapses (oil, metals) · Substitution (palladium) ·
Effective capacity returns (shipping, lumber) · Forcing function disappears (rare-earth quotas).

## Kill detector — seven derivatives

1. ΔDemand — growth decelerating 2–3 periods. 2. ΔCapacity — committed increments exceed demand increments (killed fiber).
3. Utilization 100→97→93 even with revenue intact. 4. Lead times 80→70→55→30 — best real-world variable; price may still rise while dying.
5. Inventory — first persistent rebuild. 6. Buyer elasticity Δq/Δp — early +50% price/no demand change; late +20%/−15% = nervous (rare earths).
7. Substitution investment — redesigns, thrifting, second-sourcing, recycling (palladium).

## Lifecycle

DISCOVERY (demand accelerates, supply blind) → SHORTAGE (utilization ↑, inventory ↓, lead times widen) →
RENT (prices rise, buyers don't care, margins explode) → RESPONSE (capex, entrants, redesigns) →
PEAK SCARCITY (narrative known, pipeline enormous, prices still rising) → HEALING (lead times shorten,
inventory rebuilds, utilization rolls) → COLLAPSE (price falls, margins revert, surplus).
Best entry stage 2→early 3 (memory now). SELL at stage 4→5 when second derivative flips, not when CNBC calls it.

## Most dangerous sentence

"Demand is still incredibly strong." Fiber demand grew post-2001; oil stayed indispensable; rare earths stayed
useful; shipping stayed needed; palladium stayed useful. Trade ends when scarcity rent ends, not when tech dies.

## Sector engine output (only this)

STATEMENT 1 why demand exists · 2 why input gates it · 3 why supply can't respond · 4 why buyers pay more ·
5 WHEN IT ENDS (measurable variable). Then arrows block, e.g. early: DEMAND ↑↑ UTIL ↑ INV ↓↓ LEAD ↑↑ PRICE ↑ CAP → SUB → = EARLY SCARCITY RENT;
later with PRICE ↑ but everything flipped = EXIT WARNING. **Don't predict price; predict creation and destruction of scarcity rent.**
